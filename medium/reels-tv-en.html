I Built an Instagram Reels App for Google TV — Because Scrolling Reels on a Phone Wasn't Lazy Enough

How a ~30 KB Android TV app turns your remote into a Reels controller — no private APIs, no scraping, just a WebView and a lot of key-event plumbing.

The itch

I watch a lot of Instagram Reels. What I don't love is watching them hunched over a phone. My living room has a perfectly good TV — a Chromecast with Google TV — so: why can't I just lean back and scroll Reels with the remote?

Instagram does have an official TV app now (com.instagram.airwave, February 2026), but it's US-only — in Taiwan it's not even in the Play Store. And Google TV doesn't ship a usable browser. Stuck outside the US, I built the thing myself.

The core decision: wrap the web, don't reverse the API

Rather than reverse-engineer Instagram's private API (a maintenance nightmare that violates the terms and gets accounts flagged), I load instagram.com/reels in a full-screen WebView and let Instagram's own page do the fetching and rendering. The native layer just translates the remote into the right on-page actions. From the servers, it looks like an ordinary Chrome session.

What it feels like

Remote-key mapping:

  • Up / Down — previous / next reel
  • OK (single press) — play / pause
  • OK (double press) — like / unlike
  • OK (long press) — share to a friend
  • Left — save
  • Right — open comments
  • Back — close dialog → history → exit

The interesting part: making dialogs work with a remote

Instagram's comment, share, and 2FA menus are modal overlays, and the WebView's spatial navigation lets focus escape the modal — Instagram detects that and instantly closes it. Every dialog "flashed and vanished." The fix: a MutationObserver reports modals to the app via a JS bridge, and when one is open the app takes over the D-pad, moving a blue highlight between the dialog's buttons and clicking on OK.

Four bugs that taught me something

1. The "menu opens and instantly closes" bug. The WebView synthesized a double click for a single DPAD_CENTER on Instagram's <div> buttons (open, then dismiss). Fix: on login pages, intercept OK and do exactly one deliberate click.

2. Videos default to muted, no touchscreen to fix it. Setting video.muted=false didn't stick; clicking Instagram's own unmute button does, and it stays unmuted for the session.

3. Input lag. The observer re-scanned the whole DOM on every mutation. Debouncing to 200 ms, reporting only on state changes, and capping expensive scans fixed it.

4. Reels wouldn't load on the actual TV. The logs showed codec_mm … Out of memory — the cheap Amlogic box has a tiny hardware-decoder pool, and Instagram keeps several videos decoding at once. Fix: keep only the centered reel decoding, pause the rest.

Takeaways

The lesson I keep relearning: you don't always need to rebuild the thing — sometimes you just need to re-skin how you talk to it. A WebView plus a few hundred lines of key-event plumbing gave me a lean-back Reels experience that survives Instagram's redesigns.
